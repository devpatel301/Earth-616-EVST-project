# Comparison with published findings: source matrix

Compiled 17 Nov 2026 for *Climate Change and Nutritional Security: Which Crops Are Most Vulnerable?*

How to read this file:
- **Read level.** "Full text" means I read the article body or the accepted or author manuscript, not just the abstract. Numbers marked "Table/Fig." were read from that table or figure, or from the text that reports it. "Abstract" means only the abstract was read, so use those rows for headline numbers only. "Secondary" means the number is quoted second-hand inside another source; it is flagged and should not be cited as if read directly.
- **Numbers.** Every number below appears in the source as written. The only exception is the approximate %/°C values in row K1, which are **our own derivation** and are labelled as such.
- **Scenario names.** Scenario labels are copied from each source. "CO₂ incl." means the projection includes CO₂ fertilisation. Our index does **not** include it.
- **Verbatim quotes.** Quotes are verbatim except where marked: […] marks an omission, and [reassembled] means the sentence ran across two PDF columns and was put back together in its original order.

## 3. How each could be compared with our outputs

Our index (CNVI = ½(E_heat·S_heat + E_water·S_water) × Importance) has three properties that limit every comparison:
1. **It is a ranking or relative-vulnerability score, not a yield change.** Only rank orders and signs can be compared, not magnitudes.
2. **It excludes CO₂ fertilisation, adaptation and irrigation expansion.** The fair comparators are the published *no-CO₂ or constant-CO₂* numbers: Zhao Z1–Z2, Deryng D1 "without CO₂", Jägermeyr J1 "warming sensitivity", Qian Q1 bracketed values, and Lobell L1's temperature-trend component.
3. **It uses monthly WorldClim fields and one GCM (MRI-ESM2-0, SSP2-4.5, 2041–2060).** Daily-extreme metrics (L2 degree-days > 30 °C, D1 heat stress at anthesis) measure exposure we cannot see. Multi-GCM ensembles carry spread that a single GCM does not.

Comparisons to avoid:
- Our CNVI values against any % yield number.
